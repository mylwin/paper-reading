Randomized Momentum Transport with Cheap Predictors

El Mahdi Chayti
Martin Jaggi
Machine Learning and Optimization Laboratory (MLO), EPFL

arXiv:2610.13789v1 [cs.LG] 9 Oct 2026

Abstract
Momentum methods such as STORM and
second-order momentum track the gradient
by adding a transport correction to momentum, at the cost of an extra oracle call per
iteration. We introduce randomized momentum transport, which evaluates the correction with probability q and otherwise uses
a cheap prediction, with importance weighting that preserves the conditional mean and
adds (q −1 − 1) times the prediction loss to
the variance. Under transport-bias, secondmoment, and residual-control assumptions,
linear minimization oracle (LMO) updates,
including normalized, sign, and exact spectral (Muon) directions, retain an O(T −1/3 )
stationarity bound for each fixed q > 0 with
a single-gradient initialization. For unnormalized STORM, some q < 1 lowers the leading
coefficient of our sufficient gradient budget if
and only if the gradient-difference noise modulus exceeds the smoothness constant, by at
most about 10%. An online diagonal predictor admits a regret-to-convergence guarantee.
At approximately equal oracle cost, querying a quarter of the corrections lowers the
final training loss by 18 to 43% on MNIST1D
and raises the test accuracy of Hessian-vector
transport on CIFAR-10 by 1.5 points.

INTRODUCTION

Momentum averages stochastic gradients across iterations. As the parameters move, this average must
also track the change of the gradient. STORM adds
the difference of two gradients evaluated on the same
sample at consecutive iterates (Cutkosky and Orabona,
2019); second-order momentum adds a Hessian-vector
product (HVP) (Salehkaleybar et al., 2024; Tran and
Cutkosky, 2022). Each correction costs one extra oracle
call per iteration. We study how often the correction
needs to be evaluated.

the gradient change ∇f (xt ) − ∇f (xt−1 ), and let Bt
be a cheap prediction of Ct . We use the randomized
transport estimator

Contributions.
• Randomized transport. The estimator (1)
keeps the conditional mean of the correction and
its covariance with the fresh gradient, and adds
exactly (q −1 − 1)Et ∥Ct − Bt ∥2 to its variance
(Lemma 1).
• LMO convergence. For gradient-difference and
HVP transport with LMO directions, decreasing
momentum weights, and a single-gradient initialization, the dual-norm stationarity measure of the
LMO set is O(T −1/3 ) after T iterations for fixed
q > 0. The bound holds at every T with explicit
constants; q enters only through q −1 − 1 times
the average prediction loss, and the initial error
contributes O(T −1 ) (Theorem 2, Corollary 3).
• Gradient budget of randomized STORM.
For unnormalized STORM with a zero predictor,
which uses 1 + q gradients per iteration in expectation, we give an explicit finite-horizon gradient
budget to reach expected gradient norm ε, with a
leading term of order ε−3 . Some q < 1 lowers this
term if and only if the gradient-difference noise
modulus δ exceeds the smoothness constant L, and
by at most about 10% (Theorem 4, Proposition 5).
