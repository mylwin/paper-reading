Abstract
Recent work suggests that switching optimizers between pretraining and subsequent training stages incurs a performance cost, but systematic investigation of
this phenomenon is lacking. We study the mismatch between AdamW and Muon
optimizers by pretraining 1.2B Llama-3 models from scratch while varying the proportion of updates taken with each optimizer, and similarly interpolating between
the two optimizers during continued pretraining and supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
Surprisingly, we find little evidence of a meaningful optimizer mismatch penalty:
final performance is largely explained by the independent effects of the pretraining
and post-training optimizers, with downstream performance generally improving
as the proportion of Muon updates during SFT increases. Although matching the
pretraining optimizer can reduce forgetting during SFT, this does not translate into
improved final benchmark performance. We further evaluate AdamW and Muon
for task-general SFT of open-weight models, across the Llama and Qwen families
up to 14B parameters, where Muon remains competitive with AdamW, despite
these models having been pretrained with AdamW. Together, our results suggest
that the choice of optimizer for post-training can be made largely independently of
the optimizer used during pretraining. Our code is released here.

Introduction

AdamW [Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019] has been the de facto optimizer for training most large
language models. However, recent results have shown that Muon [Jordan et al., 2024] can outperform
AdamW at scale in LLM pretraining [Liu et al., 2025, Khona et al., 2026]. Yet it remains an open
question whether Muon is also a better optimizer for supervised post-training stages, and whether
switching optimizers between training stages is detrimental to final performance. Prior work has
suggested such a penalty for using a different optimizer for SFT [Qu et al., 2026], due to differing
regularization effects [Liu et al., 2026] or implicit biases [Qu et al., 2026]. It has been unclear whether
this effect is limited to greater forgetting during task-specific fine-tuning (“forgetting cost"), or also
extends to degraded final benchmark performance (“performance cost") following the larger-scale,
multi-task SFT stage typical in foundation model training.
In this work, we examine whether there is a cost to switching between AdamW and Muon across LLM
training stages. We train our own 1.2B Llama-3 models from scratch while varying the proportion of
AdamW and Muon updates used during pretraining, continued pretraining, and task-general SFT,
allowing us to separate the effect of optimizer mismatch from each optimizer’s independent effect, as
well as that of the underlying data shift. We additionally evaluate AdamW and Muon for task-general
SFT of released open-weight models up to 14B parameters.
