Abstract
Scaling laws and hyperparameter tuning remain central challenges in training largescale models, particularly under differential privacy (DP), where optimization is
further complicated by noise injection and per-sample gradient clipping. While
Maximal Update Parametrization (µP) allows for zero-shot hyperparameter transfer
in standard training, we show that it fails under DP constraints due to the distinct
spectral properties of high-dimensional noise versus low-rank gradients. In this
work, we introduce DP-µP, a theoretically grounded framework that extends µP
to the privacy-preserving setting. By analyzing the interaction between gradient
clipping, noise injection, and feature learning, we derive a Signal-to-Noise Ratio
(SNR) condition that governs layer-wise learning rate scaling. Combining this with
our proposed spectral scaling formulation, we provide specific scaling rules for
DP-SGD, DP-Adam, and DP-Muon optimizers. Empirically, we demonstrate that
DP-µP enables effective zero-shot transfer of learning rates across model widths on
diverse architectures (MLP, ViT, GPT-2), matching or exceeding the performance
of individually tuned baselines while significantly reducing computational costs.

Introduction

The success of large-scale models such as GPT [33, 3], LLaMA [37], Qwen [41], and DeepSeek
[24], trained with billions of parameters and trillions of tokens, has underscored the importance of
effective hyperparameter tuning [19, 18, 21]. Empirically, model performance is sensitive to choices
like learning rate, momentum, and weight decay, necessitating fine-grained and computationally
intensive tuning. However, the resources used for tuning hyperparameters grow exponentially with
model size under grid or random search, posing a development for scalable model training [34, 11].
Maximal Update Parametrization (µP) [42] has emerged as a compelling solution to hyperparameter
tuning in large-scale models. By reparameterizing neural networks to preserve update magnitudes
across widths [43, 44], µP enables zero-shot transfer of hyperparameters, such as learning rate, from
small models to significantly larger ones without performance degradation. µP achieves this by
ensuring consistent layer-wise updates that stabilize activations and weights across both training
iterations and model layers. In contrast to empirical scaling laws, which can be inaccurate, modelspecific, or computationally expensive, µP offers a theoretically grounded, architecture-agnostic
framework for scalable training and robust feature learning [45].
In this work, we investigate µP in differentially private (DP) optimization [1] to enable hyperparameter
(learning rate) transfer in privacy-preserving deep learning. To put this into perspective, a well-known
empirical tradeoff exists between model utility and privacy risk. On the one hand, larger models
typically achieve better performance in tasks such as image classification and language modeling
