Spectrally Targeted Muon

Vishrut Goyal 1 Rohan Ramkumar 2

1. Introduction

Abstract

The Muon optimizer for deep learning works by orthogonalizing the update matrix (Jordan et al., 2024b). Unlike many
other proposed optimizers, this has been highly successful
empirically for training large language models, especially
when coupled with weight decay (Liu et al., 2025). This
is because the matrix can be orthogonalized relatively efficiently, using methods such as Newton-Schulz or Polar
Express (Amsel et al., 2026).

The Muon optimizer orthogonalizes each update
matrix, setting all of its singular values to one,
and has proven highly effective for training large
language models. It remains unclear, however,
whether this success comes from amplifying small
singular directions that gradient descent neglects
or from suppressing large, degenerate directions
that disrupt training. We introduce Spectrally Targeted Muon, which orthogonalizes only the singular values above or below a threshold τ , so
that varying τ interpolates between normalized
SGD and Muon. It isolates the relevant singular
subspaces with projections computed by NewtonSchulz iteration on a shifted Gram matrix, so no
SVD is needed. We evaluate these variants on
the CIFAR-10 and NanoGPT speedruns, tracking
the effective rank of gradient, update, and weight
matrices and a new metric, the alignment of updates with the tangent space of the weight matrix’s isospectral manifold. We find three things.
First, the small singular values of the momentum are not noise. Orthogonalizing everything
except the few largest singular values of each matrix nearly matches Muon while touching only a
small fraction of the momentum, whereas orthogonalizing only the top falls well short even though
it holds almost all of it. On language models every momentum singular value is far below one,
so targeted orthogonalization can only amplify,
and Muon wins by making directions that are too
small to train on at their raw scale trainable. Second, shrinking the largest singular values is what
keeps the parameter spectrum flat; this is cheap,
and it is not what drives the loss. Third, AdamW
differs from Muon mainly in how slowly it builds
structure, which explains its slower start and why
warmup helps AdamW but only hurts Muon.

The orthogonalization step can be thought of as applying the
matrix sign function to the gradient matrix, which normalizes all the singular values to one, prompting the question
of whether Muon’s success comes from amplifying small
but important directions with small singular values ignored
by standard gradient descent, or from normalizing large, degenerate directions with large singular values that misdirect
the training.
In this paper, we will explore a method to target this orthogonalization to singular values either above or below
a certain threshold τ , and we will also experiment with
different values for this threshold, thus allowing us to interpolate between SGD and Muon with a spectrally targeted
version. Specifically, we will apply these Muon variants
to the two speedrun tasks originally used to show Muon’s
performance, the CIFAR-10 dataset with a convolutional
network, and the NanoGPT large language model, just as in
Jordan et al. (2024b). In addition to standard performance
metrics, we will examine other metrics more pertinent to the
linear-algebraic structure of our weight and update matrices. This allows us to explore what characteristics of matrix
optimizers lead to richer learning.

2. Related Work
Recent Muon variants like NorMuon and Aurora mitigate
neural death in Muon via sophisticated row-wise (neuron)
normalization (Li et al., 2025; Dewulf et al., 2026). In contrast, our approach normalizes singular values to explicitly
target the weights’ underlying linear structure.
