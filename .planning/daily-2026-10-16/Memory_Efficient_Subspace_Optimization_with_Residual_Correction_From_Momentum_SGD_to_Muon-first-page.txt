A BSTRACT
Memory-efficient subspace optimization has emerged as a practical approach to
reducing the memory cost of LLM training while preserving optimization performance. Previous algorithms typically rely on the leading singular subspace of
stochastic gradients, yet the gradient noise may dominate the subspace in later
training stages and can prevent convergence. To overcome this limitation, we
adopt a residual correction framework, which maintains the optimizer states in the
subspace and adds the orthogonal residual gradient to the parameter update. We
first integrate this scheme into momentum SGD to develop SMRC, which attains
an O(T −1/4 ) convergence rate for a fixed orthonormal basis, matching that of
full-space momentum SGD. Our analysis introduces a subspace virtual iterate to
resolve the coupling between the momentum and the residual correction. Furthermore, we extend SMRC to a multi-stage variant which periodically recomputes
bases to track the evolving dominant subspace and retains the same rate. Since
each basis refresh breaks the consistency of the virtual iterates, we devise a boundary correction approach that aligns them across stages. Beyond momentum SGD,
we incorporate our framework into Muon and propose PMRC, which applies the
matrix sign to the projected momentum. Through a weighted momentum tracking
analysis that accounts for the residual gradient, we establish an O(T −1/4 ) convergence rate for PMRC, which matches the rate of full-space Muon. We also
design a multi-stage PMRC with periodically refreshed bases, which maintains a
convergence rate of the same order. Extensive experimental results confirm the
effectiveness and efficiency of our algorithms.

I NTRODUCTION

Large language models (LLMs) (Vaswani et al., 2017; Brown et al., 2020; OpenAI, 2023; Bai
et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2025; Team et al., 2025) have demonstrated strong capabilities in dialogue
(Thoppilan et al., 2022), language understanding (Chowdhery et al., 2023), mathematical reasoning
(Lewkowycz et al., 2022) and code generation (Chen et al., 2021). As model scale grows, training
imposes substantial computational and memory demands (Brown et al., 2020; Chowdhery et al.,
2023; Touvron et al., 2023). The widely used AdamW optimizer (Kingma & Ba, 2015; Loshchilov
& Hutter, 2019) further increases memory consumption, since its first- and second-moment estimates are each as large as the model itself. To alleviate the memory overhead, recent work has
explored different forms of memory-efficient training, ranging from restricting the set of trainable
parameters (Houlsby et al., 2019; Ben Zaken et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023; Pan
et al., 2024) to maintaining optimizer states in low-dimensional subspaces (Zhao et al., 2024; He
et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025a; Pan et al., 2025a). In this work, we focus on memory-efficient
