A BSTRACT
Simplicity bias, which can arise from sequential feature learning during training,
has been proposed as a general explanation for the success of deep learning. An
important driver of this phenomenon is saddle-to-saddle behavior, in which the
gradient flow trajectory remains close to a sequence of saddle manifolds while
learning one feature at a time. However, Muon, a state-of-the-art optimizer based
on spectral descent, does not exhibit such dynamics in the full-batch setting and
instead learns features in parallel. How is it then that Muon is so successful in
deep learning settings? We show that stochastic gradient noise fundamentally
changes this behavior: small-batch noise restores sequential feature learning, providing a mechanism for simplicity bias that is absent from full-batch Muon. We
explain this transition through stochastic smoothing of the spectral descent operator underlying Muon. When gradients are small relative to the noise scale, the
expected spectral update becomes approximately proportional to the actual gradient, which restores gradient-flow-like dynamics. We formalize this mechanism for
deep linear models by deriving the effective continuous dynamics of noisy spectral descent. Experiments on low-rank matrix reconstruction, vision transformers,
and large language models (LLMs) support these findings.

I NTRODUCTION

Deep neural networks are heavily overparameterized: Many different predictors can fit the training
data, yet, among the many existing solutions optimization often selects ones that generalize well.
One proposed explanation is a simplicity bias: optimization preferentially learns simpler predictors.
Sequential feature learning can produce such a bias when simpler features are learned before more
complex ones. At the level of the optimization trajectory, this behavior is often described through
saddle-to-saddle dynamics, where the trajectory moves through a sequence of increasingly complex
regions of parameter space (Pesme & Flammarion, 2023; Boursier et al., 2022; Saxe et al., 2014).
This becomes less clear once we go beyond gradient descent. Muon Jordan et al. (2024), a stateof-the-art optimizer based on spectral descent (Bernstein & Newhouse, 2025), can behave very
differently from gradient flow (Dragutinovi’c et al., 2026; Rhee et al., 2026). In simple matrixfactorization problems, gradient flow learns singular directions sequentially, whereas full-batch
Muon learns them in parallel. This is surprising: if sequential feature learning is an important
source of simplicity bias, why does an optimizer that removes this behavior work so well in modern
deep learning?
In this work, we show that the missing piece in this consideration is small-batch noise. The behavior
of Muon changes qualitatively once the gradients become stochastic. While full-batch spectral descent learns different features in parallel, small-batch spectral descent can recover a clear ordering
in which stronger features are learned before weaker ones. By learning dominant singular directions
first, these dynamics favor low-rank intermediate predictors, recovering a mechanism for simplicity
bias that is absent from deterministic Muon.
