Abstract
Distributed learning with multiple local steps reduces communication but aggravates drift, and
its practical performance often depends on carefully tuned hyperparameters under heterogeneous
data and communication constraints. At the same time, recent Muon-type optimizers suggest
that constraining updates within suitable norm balls provides a simple way to encode geometric
priors into optimization. In this work, we propose NBCLT (Norm-Ball Clipped Local Training),
a unified local-training framework for both federated learning (FL) and decentralized learning
(DL). NBCLT combines a predict-and-correct local update rule with Euclidean projections onto
flexible norm balls, thereby performing norm-ball clipping under flexible update geometries while
retaining a simple implementation. In FL, its problem-parameter-free regime achieves O(ϵ−2 )
communication complexity and O(ϵ−4 ) total stochastic-gradient complexity without assuming
bounded heterogeneity, when the per-round local-computation budget is scaled appropriately
with the horizon. In DL, NBCLT is compatible with both static and time-varying mixing and
preserves these accuracy exponents under the analogous computation scaling, up to fixed network
constants and an additive initialization cost.

Introduction

Machine learning has achieved significant progress in domains such as computer vision, natural
language processing, and scientific discovery [3, 11, 26, 27, 32]. To address requirements arising
from data privacy, system topology, and communication constraints, several distributed learning
paradigms have been developed. Federated Learning (FL) studies training over decentralized and
typically non-IID data under a central coordinator [14, 21], while Decentralized Learning (DL)
removes the coordinator and relies on peer-to-peer communication over a network [17]. Throughout
this paper, we use distributed learning to encompass both settings.
