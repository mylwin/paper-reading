Abstract
Prior work on implicit bias shows that gradient descent selects the Frobenius max-margin direction
on linearly separable problems, whereas Fan et al. (2025) established spectral max-margin behavior
for spectral-norm steepest descent methods, including idealized Muon with exact orthogonalization.
In practice, however, Muon approximates orthogonalization using finite Newton–Schulz iterations
and a positive stabilization constant. We prove that these implementation choices cause Muon
to select the Frobenius rather than the spectral max-margin direction in linear classification with
separable data. We also establish this behavior for both exponential moving average and Nesterovstyle momentum. The key technical ingredient is a summable perturbation argument showing that
the resulting parameter trajectories remain at bounded distance from those of gradient descent.
We then disentangle the roles of Newton–Schulz iteration count, the stabilization constant, and
the Newton–Schulz polynomial, revealing distinct asymptotic behaviors, including directions that
achieve neither Frobenius nor spectral max-margin. Finally, we show that Muon can recover its
spectral max-margin bias with Newton–Schulz iterations growing logarithmically over training.
Keywords: implicit bias, Muon, Newton–Schulz iterations, max-margin, stabilization

1. Introduction
Optimization algorithms can favor different solutions even when minimizing the same training
objective, a phenomenon known as implicit bias (Neyshabur et al., 2014; Gunasekar et al., 2017). A
classical example arises in linear classification with separable data, where logistic and exponential
losses can approach zero only as the parameter norm diverges. In this setting, the limiting direction
of the parameters becomes the object of interest. Gradient descent converges in direction to the
Euclidean max-margin classifier (Soudry et al., 2018; Nacson et al., 2019a; Ji et al., 2020; Ji and
Telgarsky, 2021), whereas steepest descent under different norms can select different max-margin
solutions (Gunasekar et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2023).
Muon, an optimizer that has attracted considerable attention for large language model training
(Jordan et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025), orthogonalizes matrix-valued updates rather than following
conventional gradient directions. Ignoring momentum for simplicity, its idealized update maps a
gradient matrix G = U ΣV ⊤ to its polar factor U V ⊤ . This update corresponds to steepest descent
under the spectral norm, in contrast to the Frobenius-norm geometry of standard gradient descent.
