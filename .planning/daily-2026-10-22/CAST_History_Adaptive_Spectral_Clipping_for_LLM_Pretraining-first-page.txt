Soft spectral clipping (SSC) reduces gradient
singular values, but its effect depends on the
gradient scale and clipping threshold. We introduce CAST (Clipping with Adaptive Scales
and Thresholds), a history-adaptive extension
of SSC. AdamW’s previous second moment
sets row and column scales. An exponential
moving average of the clipped output’s estimated spectral norm sets each matrix’s threshold. CAST restores the original coordinates before the AdamW update. We train a mixture-ofexperts (MoE) model with 19.08B total parameters for 83.89B tokens. CAST achieves lower
training and mean validation losses than nine
gradient-processing baselines, with the fewest
relative gradient spikes. It reaches the best
baseline’s final smoothed training loss in 7.2%
fewer steps.

Introduction

Gradient clipping(Zhang et al., 2020) controls
large gradients during language model(Team et al.,
2025b; Brown et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2024; Yang
et al., 2025; DeepSeek-AI et al., 2026; Team
et al., 2025a, 2026) pretraining (Pascanu et al.,
2013). When applied before AdamW, it also
changes the moment estimates used by later updates (Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019). The clipping
rule therefore affects both the current update and
the optimizer’s state over time.
Global norm clipping rescales the whole gradient by a single factor. This limits the influence
of an unusually large gradient while preserving its
direction. A matrix gradient can be large mainly
along a few directions. Rescaling the entire matrix
then also weakens other directions, even when their
magnitudes are already moderate. Spectral clipping controls the gradient magnitude in each singular direction. SPECTRA(Jiang et al., 2026) develops a smooth spectral transformation for appli∗

cation before or after a base optimizer(Loshchilov
and Hutter, 2019; Kingma and Ba, 2017; Jordan
et al., 2024). We use pre-optimizer SSC (SSCPre) because CAST also acts on gradients before
AdamW. We label its constant and cosine threshold schedules SSC-constant and SSC-cosine.
A clipping threshold is meaningful only relative to the gradient scale. Persistent differences in
row and column magnitudes affect the spectrum on
which clipping acts. An unusually large value in
one part of a matrix may be routine in another, and
the overall scale can change over training. A fixed
threshold may therefore apply different clipping
strengths as the gradient scale changes. SPECTRA’s decaying pre-clipping threshold shows the
value of adjusting this threshold during training.
Such a schedule depends on the training step. A
history-based rule can instead respond to changes
in each matrix. We therefore use each matrix’s history to set its gradient scale and clipping threshold.
