Abstract
Evaluating deep learning optimizer is challenging because confounding factors such as hyperparameter
tuning and optimizer-model co-design often obscure true algorithmic performance. We present results
of the updated AlgoPerf v1 benchmark, in which we evaluate a new set of training algorithms on
wall-clock time-to-target across nine workloads on fixed hardware. This report focuses on the results
of the benchmark’s self-tuning ruleset, which requires submissions to use a single configuration across
all workloads. Out of the 13 submissions in PyTorch and JAX, Schedule-Free AdamW ranked first. In
addition to the wall-clock scores, we also analyze the trade-offs between step efficiency and wall-clock
execution, revealing that matrix-preconditioned and two-loop methods achieve good sample efficiency
but require more aggressive computational optimizations to be competitive in runtime. Finally, our
hyperparameter saliency and retention analyses show that the learning rate and momentum are highly
sensitive across domains and algorithms, highlighting the need for methods that are more robust against
hyperparameter tuning and can generalize well to new tasks.

Introduction

Neural network training often relies on fragile empirical recipes governed by brittle hyperparameter tuning
procedures and massive computational budgets. Even though training algorithms, optimizers, and their
accompanying training recipes are such a crucial part of the neural network pipeline, it is very difficult to
determine what the true state of the art is in training algorithms. Much of the literature on new optimizers
suffers from confounding factors like weak baselines, optimizer-model co-design, limited testing across different
tasks and obscured tuning recipes.
The AlgoPerf: Training Algorithms benchmark [Dahl et al., 2023] was designed to largely eliminate
these confounding factors and measure practical speed-ups in neural network training achieved solely by
improving the underlying training algorithms. The benchmark was originally launched as a competition with
two rulesets. In the external tuning ruleset, submissions provided workload-agnostic hyperparameter search
spaces and then were scored based on a sample from their hyperparameter space, while in the self-tuning
ruleset, the submissions were scored without any hyperparameter sampling. In both rulesets, submissions are
compared on time-to-result across multiple deep learning workloads, training on fixed hardware. The results
of the AlgoPerf competition [Kasimbeg et al., 2025] ranked the Distributed Shampoo [Shi et al., 2023]
algorithm first on the external-tuning leaderboard, while Schedule-Free AdamW [Defazio et al., 2024] placed
first on the self-tuning leaderboard. Since then, there has been a renewed interest in second-order methods
like Muon [Jordan et al., 2024b] and momentum-based or EMA methods like SNOO [Kallusky et al., 2025]
and AdEMAMix [Pagliardini et al., 2025].
