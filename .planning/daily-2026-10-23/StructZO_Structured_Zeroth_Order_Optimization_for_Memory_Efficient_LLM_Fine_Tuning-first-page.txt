A BSTRACT
Fine-tuning large language models at scale is increasingly limited by the memory cost of backpropagation. Zeroth-Order (ZO) optimizers reduce this burden by relying only on forward function
evaluations, but they still exhibit a substantial performance gap to first-order (FO) optimizers such as
Adam. In this work, we argue that this gap does not arise solely from the absence of explicit gradients:
FO fine-tuning also benefits from informative low-rank optimization geometry, large-scale signal
aggregation, and well-conditioned update directions. Motivated by this perspective, we introduce
StructZO, a structured Zeroth-Order optimization framework for memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning.
StructZO performs large-query ZO estimation within an activation-induced informative subspace and
applies spectral control to the resulting reduced-space update before mapping it back to the original
parameters. This unified update procedure enables ZO optimization to aggregate useful directional
signals in a compact, geometry-aware space while avoiding the activation and optimizer-state memory
costs of backpropagation. We provide a theoretical analysis showing that, when the induced subspace
captures concentrated optimization-relevant directions, StructZO reduces the effective ZO estimation
dimension from mn to mr, with the remaining projection error governed by the spectral tail of the
activation Gram matrix. Empirically, across OPT-series and Llama3.1-8B on diverse downstream
fine-tuning tasks, StructZO improves over the strongest existing ZO baselines by up to 8.7% relative
on average across model settings and up to 26.9% relative on individual tasks, while remaining
within 1.5% of FO-Adam on average. These results suggest that in the evaluated fine-tuning settings,
structured and spectrally controlled ZO updates can substantially narrow the gap between ZO and FO
optimization.

Introduction

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) has become a central component in adapting large pretrained models to
downstream tasks [1]. While first-order (FO) optimizers such as Adam [2] and AdamW [3] are commonly used in
fine-tuning, they require full backpropagation through the model and therefore incur substantial memory overhead from
storing intermediate activations and optimizer states. These limitations have driven increasing interest in parameterand memory-efficient fine-tuning methods [4, 5, 6, 7]. Zeroth-Order (ZO) optimization [8], exemplified by MeZO [9],
provides a promising alternative by replacing gradient computation with forward-pass-only perturbation estimates. As
a result, MeZO reduces memory consumption close to the inference level, making full-parameter LLM fine-tuning
feasible under tight memory budgets.
Despite this memory advantage, ZO fine-tuning still suffers from slow convergence and a persistent performance
gap to first-order methods, largely due to noisy estimates in high-dimensional and ill-conditioned parameter spaces.
Since MeZO, recent work has improved ZO fine-tuning from several perspectives, including low-rank structure or
subspace [10, 11, 12], alternative sampling distributions [13, 14], momentum and variance-reduction techniques [15, 16],
multi-sample estimators [17], and Hessian- or curvature-informed updates [18, 19]. These advances show that the naive
full-space random estimator is far from optimal. However, most existing methods still focus primarily on improving the
gradient estimation itself, while remaining limited by query efficiency, search-space quality, or update conditioning.
