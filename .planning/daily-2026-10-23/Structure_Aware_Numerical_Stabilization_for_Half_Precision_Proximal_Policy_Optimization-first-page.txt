SAC-FP16 showed that algorithm-specific numerical reformulation can stabilize half-precision SAC training [3]. SAC and PPO,
however, have different objectives and data flows. SAC uses replay
and a maximum-entropy objective, whereas PPO collects an onpolicy rollout and performs multiple updates relative to the fixed

while reorganizing three error-propagation paths. Module A,
global scalar protection, stabilizes shared statistics through

persistent-update protection, builds on hAdam’s root-secondmoment representation. It introduces SHARP (Structured Halfprecision Adam with Root-state Preconditioning), which shares
second-moment state across parameter groups, and selectively
compensates FP16 writeback of the log-standard-deviation. The
residual-domain policy calculation and SHARP’s structured state
sharing are our main new designs.

Abstract
Local online training on resource-constrained platforms requires
low computational and training-state overhead. We study the numerical stability and computational efficiency of half-precision
proximal policy optimization (PPO) on commodity GPUs. Directly
converting PPO training to FP16 is unreliable because numerical
errors feed back through policy updates and subsequent data collection, while PPO repeatedly compares a current policy with a
fixed behavior policy. We propose PPO-FP16 with three structureaware modules. Global scalar protection stabilizes statistics shared
across a minibatch or parameter update. Policy-change residual
protection extracts and preserves small differences between the
current and behavior policies. Efficient persistent-update protection
stabilizes optimizer state and parameter writeback while reducing
state storage. These changes preserve the PPO objective, hyperparameters, and training budget. Across six continuous-control
tasks and five seeds, PPO-FP16 achieves the lowest mean returnAUC rank (1.90) among the four methods and ranks first in 17
of 30 environment–seed comparisons. Eight of nine performance
configurations yield 1.16–2.03× faster PPO updates. Relative to
Full FP32 Adam, optimizer-state memory falls by 71.7–74.9%, and
peak allocated GPU memory falls by up to 44.5%. The results show
that protecting a small set of structurally important quantities can
deliver stable, efficient FP16 PPO without retuning.

Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL) agents adapt their policies through
continued interaction with an environment. This ability is valuable
in robotics, autonomous systems, and control. An agent trained in
simulation may need further training after deployment to accommodate differences in hardware, sensor noise, or task conditions.
Local adaptation is especially useful when data cannot easily be
uploaded, communication adds latency, or the device must operate
independently for long periods. These resource-constrained settings motivate lower computational, memory, and energy costs for
training [37].
