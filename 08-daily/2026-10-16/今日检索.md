### 1. Memory-Efficient Subspace Optimization with Residual Correction: From Momentum SGD to Muon — 研究优先级 7.93

- **作者**：Sifan Yang, Hongyi Tao, Dingzhi Yu, Haomin Bai, Wei Jiang, Silin Gao, Pi Bu, Lijun Zhang
- **英文摘要**：

Memory-efficient subspace optimization has emerged as a practical approach to reducing the memory cost of LLM training while preserving optimization performance. Previous algorithms typically rely on the leading singular subspace of stochastic gradients, yet the gradient noise may dominate the subspace in later training stages and can prevent convergence. To overcome this limitation, we adopt a residual correction framework, which maintains the optimizer states in the subspace and adds the orthogonal residual gradient to the parameter update. We first integrate this scheme into momentum SGD to develop SMRC, which attains an $O(T^{-1/4})$ convergence rate for a fixed orthonormal basis, matching that of full-space momentum SGD. Our analysis introduces a subspace virtual iterate to resolve the coupling between the momentum and the residual correction. Furthermore, we extend SMRC to a multi-stage variant which periodically recomputes bases to track the evolving dominant subspace and retains the same rate. Since each basis refresh breaks the consistency of the virtual iterates, we devise a boundary correction approach that aligns them across stages. Beyond momentum SGD, we incorporate our framework into Muon and propose PMRC, which applies the matrix sign to the projected momentum. Through a weighted momentum tracking analysis that accounts for the residual gradient, we establish an $O(T^{-1/4})$ convergence rate for PMRC, which matches the rate of full-space Muon. We also design a multi-stage PMRC with periodically refreshed bases, which maintains a convergence rate of the same order. Extensive experimental results confirm the effectiveness and efficiency of our algorithms.

### 2. From Parallel to Sequential: Small-Batch Noise Restores Simplicity Bias of Muon — 研究优先级 7.93

- **作者**：Tom Jacobs, Rebekka Burkholz
- **英文摘要**：

Simplicity bias, which can arise from sequential feature learning during training, has been proposed as a general explanation for the success of deep learning. An important driver of this phenomenon is saddle-to-saddle behavior, in which the gradient flow trajectory remains close to a sequence of saddle manifolds while learning one feature at a time. However, Muon, a state-of-the-art optimizer based on spectral descent, does not exhibit such dynamics in the full-batch setting and instead learns features in parallel. How is it then that Muon is so successful in deep learning settings? We show that stochastic gradient noise fundamentally changes this behavior: small-batch noise restores sequential feature learning, providing a mechanism for simplicity bias that is absent from full-batch Muon. We explain this transition through stochastic smoothing of the spectral descent operator underlying Muon. When gradients are small relative to the noise scale, the expected spectral update becomes approximately proportional to the actual gradient, which restores gradient-flow-like dynamics. We formalize this mechanism for deep linear models by deriving the effective continuous dynamics of noisy spectral descent. Experiments on low-rank matrix reconstruction, vision transformers, and large language models (LLMs) support these findings.

- **英文摘要**：

Training large language models (LLMs) across geographically distributed and poorly interconnected compute pools or data centers has become a topic of significant interest. This has led to increased study of local optimization methods such as DiLoco for LLMs, especially those that use local (or inner) adaptive or preconditioned optimizers, which are critical for high-performance LLM training. However, a central challenge remains: it is well known that increasing the number of model replicas (replica scaling) while fixing the total training budget leads to monotonically decreasing performance. Existing work has empirically demonstrated that the choice of inner optimizer (Muon v.s. AdamW) can significantly improve this trend, but offers only limited explanation of the performance difference. In this work, we consider a range of inner-optimizers and identify two important factors that impact replica scaling: (1) the covariance structure of the pseudo-gradients and (2) the impact of batch size on the pseudogradient's noise scale. Based on these insights, we theoretically and empirically demonstrate that whitening-based pre-conditioning is a powerful inner-optimizer desideratum. Our empirical evaluation extends the results to reveal that whitening-based inner-optimizers achieve strong replica scaling (for 8-96 replicas), with performance relative to DP improving as parameters and data are scaled.
